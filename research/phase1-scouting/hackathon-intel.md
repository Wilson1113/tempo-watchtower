## Section A — What judges reward (evidence-backed)

- **7 internal judging dimensions** used by Colosseum staff before the panel: Founder + Market Fit, Insight, Product + Execution, Potential Market Size, Founder Communication, Viability, Traction. VERIFIED (colosseum.com/hackathon).
- **Official contest judging criteria (verbatim, rules §8)**: Functionality (quality of code), Potential Impact (TAM + ecosystem impact), Novelty, UX ("how well does this...utilize blockchain to create great UX"), Open-source ("how well does the Project Submission compose with other primitives"), Business Plan (viable future business + team's ability to execute). VERIFIED (hackathon-official-rules.txt, held locally).
- **Process**: after submissions close, Colosseum runs "multiple rounds of evaluation," advances a shortlist to the judging panel; top teams get 15-minute Zoom interviews; winners announced ~1 month after deadline (here: by 2026-12-05). VERIFIED (colosseum.com/hackathon).
- **Pitch video** (2-3 min) is "the most important element" and "usually the first item judges review" — can determine shortlisting. Must cover: team background, product description, why you started, market opportunity, how you'll get initial usage, how the product works (demo). VERIFIED (blog.colosseum.com/how-to-win-a-colosseum-hackathon/, blog.colosseum.com/perfecting-your-hackathon-submission/).
- **Technical demo video** (2-3 min, official page says "no more than three minutes") is newer: "the pitch video is for explaining the why, the technical demo is about the how." Show core features/how it works; point to docs/repo instead of going too deep. VERIFIED (blog.colosseum.com/perfecting-your-hackathon-submission/).
- **Team size signal**: "the average winning team size now above 3"; Colosseum's own guide says solo entrants are allowed but "our hackathons are highly competitive, so we recommend teaming up." VERIFIED (blog.colosseum.com/how-to-win-a-colosseum-hackathon/; colosseum.com/hackathon FAQ). This is a real headwind for a solo builder — compensate with an unusually tight, evidence-backed niche and flawless demo.
- **Founder-market fit / personal problem** is explicitly rewarded: "Build products that solve problems that affect you personally"; "teams need to start with strong founder-market fit." VERIFIED (blog.colosseum.com/how-to-win-a-colosseum-hackathon/).
- **Business viability + full-time intent** matters: "prizes will be awarded to teams who intend to build full-time and develop products with potentially viable business models." VERIFIED (same source).
- **Open-source is explicitly rewarded** by the official rules (criterion e) and repeatedly by Colosseum staff — public-good, composable, dev-tooling projects have historically won "Public Goods Award" every single hackathon (Zoneless, Attest Protocol, Zircon, IDL Space) — this is Colosseum's one guaranteed slot that specifically favors infra/dev-tools over consumer apps. VERIFIED (winners posts, cited in Section B).
- **Clay Robbins' (co-founder) investing thesis** — relevant because Colosseum-run judging culture reflects it: "We invest in founders who are early, slightly uncertain, and working on projects that they're passionate about and don't fit neatly into existing narratives" / "We invest in the people building before it makes sense" / hackathons work because they "compress time... surface ideas before they are obvious." VERIFIED (blog.colosseum.com/the-weird-corners-matter/, 31 Dec 2025).
- **On category diversity, not chasing a theme**: after Cypherpunk (9,000+ participants, 1,576 submissions) Colosseum wrote "there was no dominant theme" and framed this as a sign of ecosystem expansion, not absence of viable ideas — categories spanned "infrastructure, consumer, DeFi, stablecoins, RWAs, and a long tail of things that didn't fit neatly into any category." VERIFIED (blog.colosseum.com/crypto-is-not-cool-again/, quoting Cypherpunk data).
- **World's Fair framing (why multi-chain)**: "if Solana startups aspire to build products that reach hundreds of millions of users... they shouldn't simply compete against other Solana startups"; the goal is "not to determine which blockchain 'wins', but to increase product competition... and help crypto's grassroots startup ecosystem win as a whole." VERIFIED (blog.colosseum.com/expanding-the-arena/, 3 Sep 2026).
- **Zcash track judge expectation (Matty Taylor, Colosseum co-founder, on Zcash forum, 2026-09-03 11:47pm)**: "In this first competition, I would imagine most of the products will be utilizing ZEC the asset" and the goal is to attract "a few really high quality founders, ones who can really push the [ecosystem forward]." VERIFIED (forum.zcashcommunity.com/t/grant-proposal-colosseum-hackathon-zcash-track/57405 — fetched directly, exact quotes reproduced).
- **Skepticism from a Zcash community member (strahncryptography, same thread, 2026-09-03 11:52pm)**: worried the $250k spend would mostly buy "payment rail neutral" submissions or projects "built for BTC/ETH with simple rebranding that would never actually be used" — i.e., a senior community voice explicitly predicts a flood of low-effort, non-shielded, rebranded submissions in the Zcash track. This is a **signal of where the bar is low** for a genuinely Zcash-native (shielded-pool, FROST, Orchard/Ironwood-aware) Rust submission to stand out. VERIFIED (same thread).
- **Colosseum Copilot** (their internal AI research tool, indexing 5,400+ hackathon submissions + 84,000+ archive docs + 6,300+ Grid products) explicitly classifies competitive gaps as **full** (nobody has addressed this), **partial** (incomplete coverage by segment/UX/geography/pricing), or **false** (already solved) — this is literally how Colosseum-adjacent evaluators think about novelty. Framing your idea in exactly these terms (full/partial gap + why) will read as fluent in their own vocabulary. VERIFIED (colosseum.com/copilot, blog.colosseum.com/frontier-hackathon-colosseum-copilot-constellation-mcp/).

## Section B — Saturation map: categories to AVOID

Hard data from four past Solana hackathons (Renaissance 2024, Radar 2024, Breakout 2025, Cypherpunk 2025) plus Frontier 2026 winners lists, accelerator cohorts, and one Frontier-submission breakdown (dev.to secondary source — flagged UNVERIFIED where noted).

1. **Consumer trading / social-trading / "gamified trading" apps.** Overrepresented in every single cohort: Alpha Group Trading, Rekt, BananaZone, Daiko, Clawpump, Peaks, Bench, Fraudsworth. VERIFIED across Breakout, Cypherpunk, Frontier winner lists.
2. **Prediction-market aggregators / meta-markets.** Capitola, Synthesis, Senthos, Mentioned, attn.markets, Melee Markets — a recurring, crowded pattern across Cypherpunk and Frontier. VERIFIED.
3. **TCG / collectibles marketplaces.** JK Index, One Arena, Traded.gg, The Syndicate all funded into Cohort 5 simultaneously — signals a temporarily hot but now-saturated niche, not a gap. VERIFIED.
4. **Generic AI-agent trading/finance platforms.** Agent Arc, Clawpump, Project Plutus, theintern.fun (Breakout AI track) and "AI Platforms/Agents" was reportedly the single largest Frontier category (**493 projects / 17.2%** per a secondary dev.to-style aggregation — UNVERIFIED, could not independently confirm the exact figures from a primary Colosseum source, but multiple independent secondary sources converged on similar rank-ordering: AI > Consumer > DeFi > Developer/Infra). Also confirmed independently and primary for the **Agent Hackathon (Feb 2026)**: AI tag had 702 projects vs Infra 331 vs DeFi 291 — UNVERIFIED (search-snippet aggregation, not independently fetched from Colosseum's own dashboard).
5. **Stablecoin cross-border payment apps with no novel infra** (remittance rails, "stablecoin for X emerging market"). Multiple near-duplicate entries every hackathon: Credible, LocalPay, DashX, KinnectFi/Mana, Sp3nd, CargoBill, Decal, Xelio. VERIFIED across Breakout/Cypherpunk/Frontier lists. This is Colosseum's most repeated app pattern — a plain payments dApp with a thin Rust backend (exactly what the brief tells us to avoid) is the single most oversaturated shape of submission.
6. **Hardware wallets.** Two grand-champion-tier hardware wallet products already exist and won large prizes in consecutive hackathons: Unruggable (Cypherpunk grand champion, then Cohort 4) and the "first hardware wallet designed exclusively for Solana" (Breakout Infra track). A new hardware wallet has almost no whitespace left.
7. **Generic RPC / "faster RPC" without a specific underserved niche.** FluxRPC already won Breakout Infra ($25k) as "the first RPC on Solana that fully separates from the validator layer." A me-too general RPC provider will read as derivative.
8. **DEX/DeFi yield aggregators, LP-simplification tools, "yield search engines."** YieldCompass, MetEngine, Kormos, Watt Protocol, Hylo — repeated pattern, now a mature/crowded shape.
9. **Fantasy sports / prediction sports apps.** WeLikeSports, Crypto Fantasy League, Glympse — repeated, low differentiation.
10. **On the Zcash track specifically**: per Matty Taylor's own expectation (Section A) and strahncryptography's warning, expect a flood of "ZEC the asset" surface-level integrations (accept-ZEC-as-payment wrappers) and rebranded BTC/ETH projects with token-swap for ZEC. This is the predictable saturated shape for that track — AVOID a shallow "accept ZEC payments" dApp.

**Already funded into the Colosseum Accelerator (Cohorts 1-5) — do not resubmit adjacent ideas:**
- **Infra-flavored companies actually funded**: Rakurai (C1, Solana client/TPS), Darklake (C2, private MEV-resistant DEX), Tokamai (C2, Solana program error monitoring), Txtx (C2, onchain infra-as-code "Runbooks"), MetEngine (C3, LP automation), Slant (C3, AI research analytics for Solana), TAPEDRIVE (C3, onchain compressed storage — also Breakout Grand Champion), MCPay (C4, MCP+x402 payment middleware), Cloak (C4, ZK privacy layer for Solana DeFi), Flovia (C5, machine-paid API analytics), DashX (C5, stablecoin cross-border infra), Stablecorp (C5, founder stablecoin business infra), Zoneless (C5, open-source Stripe Connect alternative — also Public Goods winner). VERIFIED (cohort announcement posts, fetched directly).
- Takeaway: MCP/x402 agent-payment middleware, generic "Solana program monitoring," basic LP-automation, and "Stripe Connect alternative" shapes are already funded and would read as derivative if resubmitted without a sharply different angle.

## Section C — Sponsor-requested gaps that fit "solo + pure Rust + core infra"

### Solana
- **SBPFv3 / SIMD-0500 migration crunch (live, urgent, thin tooling).** Agave v4.4 (expected ~Nov 2026, i.e., **during/just after this hackathon's window**) will make SBPFv3 bytecode the *only* deployable format — new deploys/upgrades/finalizations of v0/v1/v2 programs get rejected. VERIFIED (Anza blog, colosseum.com/ar/upgrades/sbpfv3-programs, Helius Agave 4.0 blog). Two concrete, currently-thin sub-problems, both VERIFIED from a technical blog (xroot.dev, fetched directly):
  - Once the SIMD-0500 gate activates, **legacy (pre-v3) programs cannot be made immutable** (freezing/"set authority none" fails) — and `ExtendProgram` becomes **permissionless with a 10,240-byte minimum increment**, letting anyone grow (and cost) any un-migrated program's data account. Affects "a large share of the long tail: token launches from 2023/2024 with a custom program, NFT projects, small DeFi protocols, games" — i.e., an unglamorous but very real, dated, and specific population of program owners who don't know this is coming.
  - GitHub search (2026-09) turned up only two toy/demo repos for SBPFv3 migration (buffalojoec/hello-sbpfv3, soundsonacid/sbpfv3-repro) and zero repos for "SIMD-0500" tooling — genuine whitespace, not just perceived.
  - One existing partial tool: xroot.dev's token-audit page reports bytecode version + authority status for a single token/program — not a fleet-wide scanner/CLI.
- **Test Validator Plugin Framework** — an official Solana Foundation RFP (opened Oct 2023, still being discussed by community members as recently as **July 31, 2026** with no completed/awarded implementation visible) asking for a plugin system to auto-load mainnet programs/accounts into local-test-validator, "similar to docker images discovery or crates.io." VERIFIED open/unresolved as of the RFP thread's July 2026 post. A pure-Rust CLI/plugin-loader here is squarely inside "VM/async tooling," has a paper trail of demand stretching back 3 years, and remains unclaimed.
- **v1 transactions migration (Sep 2026, in-flight)**: max tx size 1,232→4,096 bytes, ALTs eliminated, compute-budget moved into a config field — breaks any indexer/tool that parses ComputeBudget-program instructions or assumes v0 tx layout. Minimum SDK bumps listed (kit 8.0.0, Rust crates 4.2.x, solders 0.29.0, Yellowstone gRPC 12.6.0/Geyser 15.1.1); **web3.js 3.x and solana-go support explicitly "still in progress."** VERIFIED (blog.colosseum.com Codex post, 27 Aug 2026). A small Rust indexer/decoder that correctly classifies legacy vs v1 fee/CU accounting is a concrete, dated niche need.
- Solana Foundation's own **Request for Startups** list (last updated Aug 2024, so dated but still the Foundation's canonical public wishlist) explicitly names **"Light RPC"** (lower-hardware RPC supporting optional indexing / read-only mode) and **"Onchain Identity"** (privacy-preserving, ZK-selective-disclosure identity) as open infrastructure asks. VERIFIED (solana.com/solutions/request-for-startups, fetched directly) — dated but not obviously fulfilled/saturated per the accelerator cohort scan above.
- Two still-technically-open, years-old Solana Foundation security-tooling RFPs (Program Verification Tooling, Pre-Deployment Program Analysis, both deadline Feb 2024) drew only a handful of applicants (CoinFabrik/Scout, Mad Shield) with **no visible award confirmation** — static-analysis / fuzzing / formal-verification tooling for Solana programs remains a named, funded-in-principle gap. VERIFIED (forum.solana.com threads, fetched directly).

### Zcash
- **zcashd hard EOL already happened**: automatic end-of-support halt hit **July 18, 2026 at block 3,417,100** — every zcashd 6.20.0 node auto-shut-down and refuses to restart. VERIFIED (zcash.github.io/zcash/user/end-of-life.html).
- **Orchard counterfeiting vulnerability (found late May 2026 by independent researcher Taylor Hornby) forced an emergency soft fork (June 2) + hard fork (June 3) + market crash (ZEC -40%+ June 4-5) + the Ironwood upgrade (NU6.3, activated July 28, 2026, block 3,428,143)**, which opened a *new* shielded pool and is closing Orchard via a turnstile (verifiable supply cap). VERIFIED (multiple 2026 news sources cross-checked; primary details from shieldedlabs.net/ironwood/ and github.com/zcash/librustzcash issue #2630, both fetched directly).
- **Concrete, dated, unassigned Rust engineering task**: `zcash_pool_migration` (Orchard→Ironwood wallet migration engine) — GitHub issue opened by nuttycom (Electric Coin / librustzcash) **2026-07-15**, fully speced (fund-splitting into denominations, PCZT building/signing, anchor management, a five-phase state machine) and explicitly **"no individual currently assigned to this work"** as of the fetch. VERIFIED directly from github.com/zcash/librustzcash/issues/2630. This is about as clean a "sponsor-requested, unclaimed, pure-Rust cryptography/wallet-infra gap" as exists in this entire research pass.
- Community migration tooling landscape is thin and largely community-run: ironwood.live tracker is explicitly "**not affiliated with ECC, ZF or Shielded Labs**," built by one person, and was showing "STALE DATA" at time of fetch — i.e., even the *tracking* dashboard for a $1.8B+ asset migration is a fragile side-project. VERIFIED (ironwood.live, fetched directly).
- Z3 stack dashboard (zecdev.org) itself states "this is the first version of what will become a Dashboard" — Zaino listed as "catching up" (not on-track) as of **July 19, 2026**. VERIFIED.
- **ZCG wishlist (evergreen)**: explicitly names "easy multi-sig tools for Shielded Addresses (e.g. an implementation of FROST in user-facing wallets)" and lightwalletd infra/maintenance. VERIFIED (search-derived, cross-checked against ZecHub Hackathon 3.0 "FROST" track existing exactly to fill this — meaning FROST-wallet UX is a named ask but ALSO already the single most active ZecHub hackathon track (CYZE, ZHAC, Steward, Konclave, FrostVault, ZecVault all shipped in Aug 2026) — **treat generic "FROST wallet" as increasingly crowded within the Zcash community itself**, even though the Colosseum/Solana-native audience won't have seen it.
- **NU7 scope locked in Feb 2026**: only 4 features have cross-community consensus — Project Tachyon (universal support), Orchard Quantum Recoverability (~90-95%), Explicit Fees (~83-87%), NSM fee-burning (~78-80%). **ZSAs (Zcash Shielded Assets) were rejected by coinholders 97.3% opposed** despite 77.9% ZCAP support — meaning a "build a ZSA-powered product" idea is fighting the base's own explicit rejection, a strong reason to avoid it. VERIFIED (zfnd.org/nu7-polling-results-what-we-heard-and-where-we-go-from-here/, fetched directly, dated Feb 23 2026).
- Zakura (new high-performance Zcash full node, led by Sean Bowe + Dev Ojha/Valar Group, targeting 50,000 TPS) explicitly states "**the current stack won't get us there**" and frames the gap as **networking/consensus-software performance, not cryptography** — i.e., an async/networking Rust specialist (not just a cryptographer) has a stated, credible opening here. VERIFIED (zakura.com/announcements/introducing-zakura/, fetched directly).

### Tempo
- Mainnet live since **March 18, 2026**; MPP (Machine Payments Protocol, co-developed with Stripe) already has an **official Rust SDK** (`tempoxyz/mpp-rs`, also on crates.io) — a generic MPP client/server gateway is already covered, don't rebuild it. VERIFIED.
- **Tempo Zones (privacy)** — TEE-based (AWS Nitro Enclaves) private execution environments, announced with a blog post **2026-09-23** (one day before this research), currently in **closed access, first Zone launching October 2026 for one customer only**. Explicitly open-source software that "we can help you run one on your own infrastructure," and explicitly invites zone-operator integrations, wallet-provider integration (e.g. via Privy), and compliance-policy tooling (TIP-403). VERIFIED (tempo.xyz/developers/blog/privacy-with-tempo-zones, fetched directly). This is the freshest, most concretely-invited gap in the whole Tempo ecosystem right now — but note it explicitly excludes smart-contract support ("designed only to support payments") and forced-withdrawal/operator-failure recovery is still "in development," meaning a pure-Rust **Zone-operator toolkit, or an independent auditor/verifier of TEE attestations for a Zone**, is unclaimed and squarely infra/cryptography.
- **Ecosystem-infrastructure directory gaps (snapshot 2026-09-14, tempoatlas/awesome-tempo)**: no dedicated entries exist for **Rust SDKs (beyond the official one), light clients, payment-channel/MPP server tooling, travel-rule compliance infra, reconciliation/accounting tools, privacy solutions, or node-monitoring tools** — RPC/indexer/wallet/bridge/oracle/compliance-scanner categories are already saturated with named incumbents (Alchemy, QuickNode, Dune, Chainlink, Chainalysis, etc.), so competing head-on there is unwise; the explicitly-empty categories above are the honest whitespace. VERIFIED.
- Tempo's own **stable-bench-v1** (an eval for whether coding agents can build Tempo apps) is open-source and explicitly invites contributions from "eval practitioners" — low-signal for a core-infra idea but shows Tempo is developer-tooling-friendly and receptive to third-party OSS contributions being visible/credited.
- T7 upgrade (July 2026) introduced per-payer/per-maker storage credits tracked via a new `TIP20ChannelReserve` precompile for MPP channels — a **payment-channel reserve/credit accounting monitor or reconciliation tool** (matching the explicit "reconciliation/accounting" gap above) is a concrete, dated, narrow Rust infra idea.

### Hyperliquid
- **HIP-3 (builder-deployed permissionless perps) operational burden is real and documented**: deployers must run an oracle updater publishing **every 3 seconds** (no more than 2.5s between calls), with a **10-second stale-window fallback to local mark price that the docs explicitly warn against relying on**; must stake **500,000 HYPE for a minimum 183 days**; and public Hyperliquid endpoints are **rate-limited to 100 requests/minute/IP — "not close to fine for operating a live DEX."** VERIFIED (chainstack.com/hyperliquid-hip3-builder-deployed-perpetuals-trust-boundary/, 29 Jul 2026, fetched directly).
- BUT: this gap is **already being actively filled by well-funded incumbents** — Pyth ships "HIP-3 as a Service" (full managed oracle+infra+capital solution) and RedStone shipped "Hyperstone," its first HIP-3 oracle (100M+ updates in testnet). A solo Rust builder competing directly on "HIP-3 oracle infra" is now fighting two funded oracle networks — reclassify this from "gap" to "big-co already moved in," i.e. AVOID a generic HIP-3-oracle-as-a-service pitch.
- **Narrower, still-open sub-niche**: independent, low-cost **monitoring/alerting tooling *for* HIP-3 deployers** (watching the 10-second stale window, oracle-uptime, open-interest caps, and the "deployer submitted a correct representation of the external market" trust boundary that "consensus cannot verify independently") is not the same product as an oracle network itself, and no incumbent was found explicitly filling this monitoring/attestation-of-deployer-honesty niche.
- Foundation opened a **paid low-latency data-access tier "under $1,000/month"** in Aug 2026 for qualified infra providers (replacing the old 10,000 HYPE stake + Tier-1 maker-volume requirement) — meaning direct low-latency access is now commercially available but still gated behind provider qualification criteria (1yr history, 100+ customers, 5+ networks, 99.9% uptime) that a solo hackathon entrant cannot meet in 14 days; a solo builder would have to build *on top of* a qualified provider (e.g. via existing order_book_server or a Quicknode-style reseller), not replace it.
- The open-source `hyperliquid-dex/order_book_server` (Rust, last updated **July 15, 2026**) already provides local L4/BBO/L2 book streaming co-located with a node — a strong existing primitive; differentiate from it rather than duplicate it (e.g., it explicitly does **not** support spot order books and does **not** show untriggered trigger orders — two named, narrow, real gaps in an otherwise-solid existing tool).

### Ethereum L1 / Base / Arbitrum / Robinhood Chain
- **Ethereum Foundation's 2026 protocol priorities (Sep 7, 2026 post, fetched directly)**: five research arcs — Post-Quantum (primary focus, target full quantum resistance across all 3 layers by **Dec 2029**), Fast Finality, Privacy ("privacy as a protocol guarantee"), State growth, zkEVM (moving proofs "available→expected→mandatory"). Explicit call: **"Delivering PQ-readiness will also take more hands than any prior fork sequence... this will be an ecosystem-wide effort."** VERIFIED. However: **ESP's own Wishlist and RFP pages currently show zero active items** ("There are currently no active wishlists/RFPs available for application") as of this research pass — so there is no live, funded, specific EF ask to point to right now; the priorities post is directional, not a concrete bounty. This makes an EF-track pitch harder to evidence than Solana/Zcash/Tempo, where dated concrete gaps exist.
- **Base migrated its entire node stack to Rust/Reth** (`base/base` repo, single binary, `base-reth-node` + `base-consensus` replacing op-geth/op-node); Sepolia activation **2026-04-20**, mainnet **TBD as of research date**. After activation, `op-node`, `op-geth`, standalone `op-reth`, and Nethermind configs **stop syncing canonically** — a real, dated, narrow infra event (indexers/node operators must migrate) that a Rust builder could target (e.g., a migration-readiness checker, or an `optimism_*`-namespace compatibility shim/monitor), though Base's own team is clearly already building the primary replacement stack, so scope must be a genuinely orthogonal tool, not a competing node.
- **Robinhood Chain mainnet went live July 1, 2026** on Arbitrum Orbit/Nitro (chain ID 4663; testnet 46630), ETH gas, Chainlink price feeds on Stock Tokens. Stock Tokens are **plain ERC-20 with no special extensions** — and critically, the fetched guide found **zero documentation of transfer-restriction/allowlist mechanics, zero documented onchain handling of corporate actions (dividends/splits), and no Stylus support mentioned anywhere.** VERIFIED gaps (quicknode.com guide, fetched directly). This is a genuine, concrete, freshly-launched (11 weeks old) infra gap: **no on-chain, protocol-level mechanism for corporate actions (dividends, splits) on tokenized-equity rails** is a core-infra cryptography/state-management problem, not a UI problem, and nobody is visibly solving it yet.
- **Arbitrum Stylus** (Rust/WASM smart contracts) has an active **$5M ARB "Stylus Sprint"** grant program and a maturing SDK (`stylus-sdk-rs`); this is the most explicitly pure-Rust-friendly EVM-adjacent track of the eight, and Stylus + Robinhood-Chain-corporate-actions is a plausible pure-Rust intersection (build a Stylus contract library handling dividend/split logic for tokenized-equity issuers) worth a candidate idea.

### C3 — SBPFv3 Fleet Migration Scanner (working name: **sbpf-fleet**)

- **Problem**: SIMD-0500 (expected with Agave 4.4, ~Nov 2026) will (a) block all new deploys/upgrades of pre-v3 programs and (b) make legacy programs permanently un-freezable while simultaneously exposing them to permissionless, attacker-funded account-size griefing via ExtendProgram. Most affected teams have no idea this is coming.
- **Evidence**:
  1. xroot.dev/blog/solana-legacy-programs-cannot-be-made-immutable-simd-0500 — "Anyone with a deployed program built before the v3 toolchain who has not upgraded since... describes a large share of the long tail." VERIFIED (fetched directly).

- **Why big players won't do it**: Anza/Solana Labs ship the compiler/toolchain and documentation, not a fleet-wide scanner for other people's already-deployed programs — it's exactly the kind of "long-tail small-program owner" support work that's beneath a protocol team's roadmap but valuable to the ecosystem.

- **Business model sketch**: free CLI (open-source, judging-criterion-aligned) + paid "compliance report" service for protocols/DAOs needing to certify their program fleet is SIMD-0500-safe ahead of the Agave 4.4 activation.
